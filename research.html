Research

How do political and social orders emerge, endure and change after violence? My research explores this question through the institutions, relationships and norms that shape peace and security.

I work across peace and conflict studies, comparative politics and international relations. Much of my research draws on post-conflict societies in sub-Saharan Africa, connecting institutional change with people's everyday experiences of security, legitimacy and peace.

Research themes

01

Peacebuilding and post-conflict institutions

What makes institutional reform contribute to sustainable peace? I study security sector reform, state-society relations and the political choices that shape institutions after war. My work examines the gap between formal reforms and their effects on people's lives.

02

Security, trust and legitimacy

Why do people trust some security institutions but distrust others? I examine policing, peacekeeping and security governance, including the ways international interventions shape relationships between citizens and the state.

03

Forgiveness, reconciliation and social relations

How do people live together after violence, especially when justice or closure remains out of reach? This research explores forgiveness, the rebuilding of relationships and the possibility of coexistence without reconciliation.

04

International norms and institutional change

How do international norms and institutions lose authority or become undone? I am interested in norm contestation, deinstitutionalisation and the political processes through which established international commitments change.

Current and emerging questions

My ongoing research brings together three lines of inquiry: trust in police and security institutions; forgiveness and social relationships after conflict; and the contestation and dismantling of international norms and institutions.

Although these projects address different levels of politics, they share an interest in how relationships and institutions are sustained-or transformed-when established expectations break down.

Research in context

My work has examined security sector reform, police reform in peace agreements, peacekeeping, and institutional reform after armed conflict. Research on the UN mission in Mali asks how international peacekeeping affects trust in national police.

Research based on fieldwork in Northern Uganda considers how people navigate the legacies of violence, including relationships that continue without forgiveness or closure.
