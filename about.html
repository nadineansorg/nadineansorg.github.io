About me

I am a political scientist working at the intersection of peace and conflict studies, comparative politics and international relations. I study how institutions and societies respond to violent conflict. I am particularly interested in security, legitimacy and how people rebuild relationships after violence.

I grew up in East Germany, and the fall of the Berlin Wall is my earliest political memory. It taught me how quickly political systems can change and how much democracy and freedom matter. These values have shaped my academic journey and continue to guide my work. They also underpin my interest in political change and the ways institutions affect people's lives.

Much of my work has focused on post-conflict societies, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. I am interested in how formal institutions shape people's everyday experiences of peace and security. Why do people trust—or distrust—the police? How do institutional reforms affect political and social relationships? And how do societies live with legacies of violence that remain unresolved?

More recently, I have explored forgiveness and reconciliation, alongside the contestation and transformation of international norms and institutions. Across these projects, I ask how political and social orders emerge, endure and change.

Academic background

I am based at Corvinus University of Budapest, having previously worked at the University of Kent in the United Kingdom. I completed my PhD in Political Science at Freie Universität Berlin.

My academic career combines research and teaching with academic leadership. At Kent, I served as Head of the School of Politics and International Relations from 2021 to 2023, alongside other roles in postgraduate education and academic governance.

I value interdisciplinary collaboration, intellectual exchange and academic environments that encourage curiosity, critical debate and openness to different perspectives.

Research and teaching

My teaching covers peacebuilding, security studies, conflict analysis, mediation and negotiation, and international relations. I enjoy helping students connect theory to real-world political challenges. I encourage them to question established assumptions and develop their own arguments.

I supervise doctoral research in peace and conflict studies, security governance, institutional change and related fields.
