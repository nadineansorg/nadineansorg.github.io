About me

I am a political scientist working at the intersection of peace and conflict studies, comparative politics and international relations. My research examines how institutions and societies respond to violent conflict, how security and legitimacy are negotiated, and what enables people and communities to rebuild relationships in the aftermath of violence.

Growing up in East Germany, the fall of the Berlin Wall was my first formative political memory. Experiencing this moment of profound political transformation shaped my understanding of the importance of democracy and freedom, values that continue to guide both my personal convictions and my academic journey. It also inspired my enduring interest in how political orders change, how institutions shape people's lives, and how societies navigate profound political and social transformations.

Much of my work has focused on post-conflict societies, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa. I am interested in the relationship between formal institutions and everyday experiences of peace and security: how people develop or lose trust in security institutions, how institutional reforms shape political and social relationships, and how societies navigate legacies of violence that are not easily resolved.

More recently, my research has expanded towards questions of forgiveness and reconciliation, as well as the transformation and contestation of international norms and institutions. Across these different projects, I am interested in how political and social orders are established, sustained and transformed.

Academic background

I am based at Corvinus University of Budapest, having previously worked at the University of Kent in the United Kingdom. I completed my PhD in Political Science at Freie Universität Berlin.

My academic career has combined research and teaching with leadership and institutional development. At Kent, I served as Head of the School of Politics and International Relations from 2021 to 2023, alongside other roles in postgraduate education and academic governance.

I value interdisciplinary collaboration, intellectual exchange and academic environments that encourage curiosity, critical debate and openness to different perspectives.

Research and teaching

My teaching covers peacebuilding, security studies, conflict analysis, mediation and negotiation, and international relations. I particularly enjoy helping students connect theoretical debates with real-world political challenges, question established assumptions and develop their own analytical perspectives.

I supervise doctoral research in peace and conflict studies, security governance, institutional change and related fields.

Beyond academia

Outside academia, I am a keen long-distance cyclist and enjoy exploring new places, often by bicycle. Travelling through unfamiliar landscapes, meeting people along the way and spending time outdoors are important parts of my life.

I have a deep love for animals and care strongly about their wellbeing. This is also reflected in my vegan lifestyle and my enjoyment of discovering interesting vegan food wherever I go.
